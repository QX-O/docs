## The extracted question

The notes say anger gives a foothold into your mind for Satan.

## Short answer

Anger becomes a foothold when it is nursed, justified, exaggerated, or left unresolved. It gives the enemy room to intensify bitterness, suspicion, revenge, slander, and division.

## Anger is not always sin

Scripture can speak of righteous anger. The problem is not every experience of anger; the problem is anger that rules the heart and becomes an excuse for sin.

## How anger becomes access

### It rehearses offense

Anger often replays the wound until the person is no longer responding to what happened but to an enlarged story of what happened.

### It invites accusation

The enemy is called an accuser. Unresolved anger can turn the mind into a courtroom where another person is constantly prosecuted.

### It divides community

Anger spreads through speech. Venting can become slander when it recruits others into bitterness instead of seeking truth and peace.

### It hardens identity

A person can begin to identify as the wounded one, the betrayed one, or the one who must never forgive. That identity can compete with identity in Christ.

## How to close the foothold

1. Bring the anger into prayer without pretending.
2. Separate facts from assumptions.
3. Refuse revenge.
4. Forgive as an act of obedience, even when trust must be rebuilt slowly.
5. Seek reconciliation when wise and safe.
6. Get pastoral or professional help when anger is tied to trauma, abuse, or ongoing danger.
